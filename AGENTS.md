- Info scenes are distinct from the named Vibe/performance presets: one combined Clock + Weather scene, Set Times, and signal-driven Waveform replace GIF rendering while active. The combined scene defaults to a bright daytime, warm dusk, or dark night sky from approximate time windows (6–8 AM and 6–8 PM are dusk; 8 AM–6 PM is day), regardless of clouds, and a single Black choice overrides the whole scene. The twofold pixel clock reads in 24-hour time with a real colon; temperature and conditions fit beneath it. Selecting the scene syncs clock time and requests current festival weather; if weather fails it displays the last saved or missing reading while the clock continues. The phone sends UTC time plus the America/Chicago offset (including DST); the board keeps time from monotonic ticks, resynced each minute while an information scene is active and the page is open. A cold boot has no trusted time until phone sync. The phone can request current weather from Open-Meteo at the festival venue without GPS, or optionally via phone GPS on a secure browser origin; both require phone internet access. Manual weather entry and last saved weather remain available offline. The browser stores four independent 2026 Wakaan nights and restores them on page load; until official day assignments arrive the official alphabetical roster is an unassigned pick list. A night can hold acts without times; enter times to advance by clock, including sets after midnight (before 9 AM count for the previous night). The schedule is not persisted on the board. The waveform uses normalized Energy/Low/Body/Bright signals. Text and icons share Black, Dimmed GIF (65% brightness), and None backgrounds; audio brightness response is shared while text glyph geometry stays fixed.
- The shared runtime has a selectable mirror mode: render only the front, copy its final pixels to the back, and close the back GIF decoder when supported by the media adapter. This preserves front/back controls for independent mode. Mirroring is selectable in Manage → Setup, not automatically enabled on the physical board; measure it against independent playback before treating savings as verified.

## Power and wiring decision (latest)

The working plan is UGREEN USB-C PD battery → PD trigger → TOBSUN EA50-5V 50 W buck → main fuse → DC switch → 5 V distribution. The two panel power connectors each receive their own parallel 5 V/GND branch. The user has **ordered a 5 V/5 A USB-C male-to-bare-wire power pigtail**, intended as a **third 5 V/GND distribution branch feeding the MatrixPortal S3 USB-C input**. This supersedes the older separate-battery-port controller feed described in previous revisions of `HARDWARE_ARCHITECTURE.md`. Cable rating is capacity, not imposed board current. Verify cable polarity, actual 5 V output, USB-C power-in behavior and branch protection on the bench before final assembly. Use the buck's **output** terminals for all 5 V branches; the PD-trigger side is higher voltage. The buck label says **12 V/24 V input, 5 V 10 A max output**; the trigger offers 5/9/12/15/20 V. Confirm the converter's supported input range and actual PD trigger voltage under load before using 20 V; the photo label alone does not settle intermediate-voltage compatibility. A 5 V/10 A supply may be tight for two bright panels, so cap brightness and measure power. The MatrixPortal's panel mounting terminals are USB-fed **outputs**, not the controller power input. Do not power the controller through HUB75.

The intended parts also include a main fuse (8–10 A was proposed, **not a validated final size**), DC-rated switch and distribution block. A branch fuse suitable for the controller pigtail is desirable because a system fuse sized for panels may not protect a thin branch. Detailed build/testing sequence is in `HARDWARE_ARCHITECTURE.md`.
